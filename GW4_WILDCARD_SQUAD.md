## How I picked them

I used your four rules in the order you gave them.

**Rule 1: minutes.** I only looked at players who average 60 minutes or more per game. That cut the pool from 654 players down to 184. It is the strongest filter, because a player who does not play cannot score.

**Rule 2: form.** I ranked what was left by form, which is the average points a player has scored recently.

**Rule 3: goalkeepers and defenders.** Where form was close, I preferred the lower expected goals conceded. That favours players behind a defence that gives up fewer good chances.

**Rule 4: midfielders and forwards.** Where form was close, I preferred the higher expected goal involvement. That favours players who create and finish more chances.

Then I made sure the squad fitted the budget, the position limits, and everything you asked for below.

## Where two of your rules disagree

You asked me to follow the top five managers' spending. You have also learned, the hard way in Gameweek 2, that going against the players everyone owns costs you when they deliver.

This week those two ideas point in opposite directions.

Here is what the top five spend on their starting eleven, next to this squad.

| Position | Top five average | This squad | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goalkeeper | £5.1m | £4.6m | −£0.5m |
| Defence | £17.6m | £14.6m | −£3.0m |
| Midfield | £36.3m | £33.1m | −£3.2m |
| Attack | £21.1m | £30.7m | **+£9.6m** |

The squad spends about £10m more on attack than they do. That is because it keeps Haaland at £15.5m.

**I built the alternative so you can see the cost.** A squad that matches their spending almost exactly needs to drop Haaland. It gets attack down to £22.9m and midfield up to £37.4m. But it loses **10.6 points of form** across the eleven, and it gives up a player that **19 of the 22 managers in your league own**.

In a 22-manager league your rank depends on those 22 people, not on the whole game. Dropping an 86%-owned player is the single biggest risk you can take in a week. When he hauls, you lose ground to nearly everyone at once.

**So I kept Haaland and recommend you do too.** The spending template is a good guide over a season. The ownership template is what decides single weeks, and this squad still moves you toward the top five in the other three positions.

## Two honest costs

**Konsa costs you form.** You told me you were comfortable with this, and I have kept him. But here is the size of it: leaving Konsa out and picking the best available defender instead would raise the eleven's form by **6.6 points**. He has played 101 minutes all season and his form is 1.7, the lowest of anyone in the squad.

There is also a knock-on effect. Because your bench is fixed at four other names, Konsa cannot sit on the bench. He has to start. If you would rather bench him, swap him with Bobby Thomas, who has a form of 5.0. That single change lifts the eleven without touching anything else.

**Tzolakis is a form pick, not a safety pick.** He tops every goalkeeper on form by a wide margin, 8.7 against 5.0 for the next best, so your rules select him clearly. But he plays for Hull, a promoted club, and his expected goals conceded of 1.68 is high. Your rules put form first and I followed them. Just know that you are buying recent scoring, not a solid defence.
