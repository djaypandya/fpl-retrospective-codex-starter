## 1. What you fixed, and what was left to choose

You locked in six players and a bench. That committed **£67.5m of your £100.6m**.

| Locked in | Cost |
|---|---|
| Haaland, Palmer, João Pedro, Szoboszlai, Calafiori, Konsa | £49.9m |
| Bench: Kinsky, Janelt, Egan, Thomas | £17.6m |
| **Total committed** | **£67.5m** |

That left **£33.1m and exactly five slots**: one keeper, one defender, two midfielders, one forward. Because your bench holds a keeper, two defenders and a midfielder, the shape is forced to 3-4-3.

The five open slots went to the highest form available: **Tzolakis, Bogle, Gakpo, Scott and Wissa.**

Isak and Bruno Fernandes were removed from the pool entirely, as you asked.

## 3. What dropping Bruno costs you

Bruno Fernandes has the second-highest form in the game at 9.0, so leaving him out has a price. But it is smaller than you might think, because you replaced him with Gakpo, who is on 9.3.

The real effect is on your money. Bruno costs £12.0m. Without him, that money went into Palmer at £9.6m and better cheap players elsewhere.

**In your league, 10 of 22 managers own Bruno.** So you are taking a moderate risk against them, not a severe one. Compare that to Haaland at 19 of 22, where being out would be brutal.

## 4. Why £4.6m sits in the bank

This is the part worth reading carefully, because it looks like a mistake and is not.

I tested what happens if you force all the money to be spent:

| Money spent | Form across the eleven |
|---|---|
| **£96.0m (this squad)** | **73.6** |
| £99.0m | 72.6 |
| £99.6m | 69.6 |
| £100.1m | 69.0 |

**Spending more money buys worse form.** The players in the best form right now happen to be cheap: Tzolakis at £4.6m, Bogle at £4.5m, Scott at £6.0m. Forcing another £4m into the squad pushes those out and pulls in more expensive players who are not scoring as well.

You asked me to maximise form. Spending everything would break that rule to hit a number that does not matter.

Money in the bank is also useful. It funds a future transfer without needing a downgrade elsewhere, and it protects you against price rises. Going into an unknown run of fixtures with £4.6m spare is flexibility, not waste.

## 5. Two things to weigh before you confirm

**Konsa still costs you.** He has played 101 minutes all season and his form is 1.7, the lowest in the squad by some distance. And because your bench is fixed at four other names, **he cannot be benched, he has to start.** If you want him out of the eleven, swap him with Bobby Thomas, who is on form 5.0. Everything else stays put.

**This squad is light in defence and heavy in attack.** Your split is £4.6m keeper, £14.6m defence, £29.8m midfield, £29.4m attack. The 15 elite managers I benchmarked average £5.3m, £17.2m, £36.4m and £21.5m. You are about £8m over them in attack and about £7m under in midfield.

That is a direct consequence of naming Haaland at £15.5m, and it is a legitimate choice. Just know that you are deliberately going the other way from the managers with the best records, and that the earlier reports have flagged the midfield gap since Gameweek 1.

## 6. If you relax one rule

Letting yourself take three players from one club, which every one of the 15 elite managers does, raises the eleven's form from **73.6 to 75.6**. It swaps Bogle and Wissa for Ajayi of Hull and Havertz of Arsenal.

It is a small gain and it does concentrate your risk. Your call, but the option is there and the elite managers all take it.
